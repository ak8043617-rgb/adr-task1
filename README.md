# Task 1: ADR Identification Basics

## Patient scenario (as shown on the task card)
- **Patient:** Female, 45 years
- **Presenting symptoms:** Rash, itching, nausea, dizziness
- **Medication history:**
  - Amoxicillin, started 5 days ago
  - Paracetamol, as needed
- **Timeline:**
  - Day -5: Amoxicillin started
  - Day -2: Symptoms began (about 3 days after the first dose)
  - Day 0: Patient presents with symptoms

## Step 1: Review symptoms and history
The patient developed a rash and itching (skin and hypersensitivity signs) together with nausea and dizziness. These appeared after a new drug was started, with no other new medicine in the history.

## Step 2: Analyze the medication history and timeline
| Drug | Started | Temporal relationship | Suspicion |
|------|---------|----------------------|-----------|
| Amoxicillin | Day -5 | Symptoms began 3 days after the first dose | **Suspected drug** |
| Paracetamol (PRN) | Long-standing / as needed | No clear new start, rarely causes this picture | Concomitant drug, low suspicion |

Amoxicillin is a penicillin. Skin rash and itching are well-documented reactions to it, and nausea is a common side effect. The onset (about 3 days after starting) fits a drug reaction.

## Step 3: Is the reaction drug-related?
**Yes, it is probably an Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR).**

- **Temporal association:** the reaction began after amoxicillin was started.
- **Known reaction profile:** rash and itching are labelled adverse effects of amoxicillin.
- **Classification:** most likely a **Type B (unpredictable)** reaction, i.e. a **hypersensitivity (allergic)** reaction. The nausea may be a dose-related **Type A** effect.
- **Hypersensitivity type:** a rash appearing a few days after starting is typical of a delayed or non-IgE-mediated reaction (Type IV), though an early IgE-mediated (Type I) reaction cannot be ruled out without more detail.

## Causality assessment

### Naranjo scale (based only on the information given)
| # | Question | Answer | Score |
|---|----------|--------|-------|
| 1 | Previous conclusive reports of this reaction? | Yes | +1 |
| 2 | Event appeared after the drug was given? | Yes | +2 |
| 3 | Improved when the drug was stopped? | Not known yet | 0 |
| 4 | Reappeared on re-administration? | Not done | 0 |
| 5 | Alternative causes that could explain it? | Not known | 0 |
| 6 | Reaction with placebo? | Not known | 0 |
| 7 | Drug detected at toxic concentration? | Not known | 0 |
| 8 | Reaction worse with higher dose? | Not known | 0 |
| 9 | Similar reaction to the same or similar drug before? | Not known | 0 |
| 10 | Confirmed by objective evidence? | Not known | 0 |
| | **Total** | | **3 = Possible** (1-4) |

If the rash improves after stopping amoxicillin (+1) and other causes such as a viral infection are excluded (+2), the score reaches 6, which is **Probable** (5-8).

### WHO-UMC category
**Possible.** There is a reasonable time relationship and the reaction is known for this drug, but dechallenge information is missing and other causes are not yet excluded. It becomes **Probable/Likely** if the reaction resolves after withdrawal and no other explanation fits.

## Step 4: Identify the suspected drug
**Suspected drug: Amoxicillin**
Paracetamol is a concomitant medication and is unlikely to be responsible.

## Severity and warning signs
Rash with dizziness and nausea can be an early sign of a more serious reaction. The clinician should check for facial or throat swelling, breathing difficulty, wheeze, low blood pressure or widespread blistering or skin peeling. Any of these means **emergency treatment**.

## Recommended actions
1. Stop amoxicillin and have a clinician review the patient. Do not restart it without medical advice.
2. Treat symptoms as advised by the clinician (for example an antihistamine for itching).
3. Record "suspected penicillin/amoxicillin allergy" in the patient's record and tell the patient to inform future prescribers.
4. Report the ADR through the national pharmacovigilance system (in India, the PvPI/CDSCO ADR reporting form or the ADR PvPI mobile app).

## Impact on patient safety
Identifying and reporting this reaction prevents re-exposure to the same drug or related penicillins, and adds to the national drug-safety database.
